## [Guest Lecture](https://aselshall.github.io/gwh/hw/guest-lecture)

### 1. Purpose

Guest lectures provide an opportunity to hear how groundwater concepts are used in research, engineering practice, environmental management, and public decision-making. The purpose of this assignment is not simply to summarize a lecture, but to connect the speaker’s ideas to the technical concepts studied in this course.

### 2. Instructions

Select and watch one Guest Lecture video posted in Canvas. Then prepare a Guest Lecture Response containing the following four sections.

#### 1. Lecture Information (5 points)

Provide,
- Name of the guest speaker
- Speaker’s organization or professional role
- Title or main topic of the lecture
  
#### 2. Three Important Takeaways (20 points): 

Identify three ideas, findings, methods, or professional insights that you found important or exciting.  Explain the idea in your own words. You my also reflect on why the idea is important to groundwater hydrology or water-resource management.

Recommended length: 100–200 words total

#### 3. Connection to the Course (20 points)

Explain how the lecture connects to at least two course topics, such as:
- Water budgets
- Aquifer properties
- Hydraulic conductivity
- Hydraulic head and gradients
- Darcy’s law
- Groundwater–surface water interaction
- Pumping and aquifer response
- Aquifer testing
- Hydrogeologic site characterization
- Groundwater modeling
- Contaminant transport
- Groundwater management

Do more than state that the topics are related. Explain the technical connection using terminology, equations, figures, examples, or principles from the course.

Recommended length: 100–200 words

#### 4. Question for the Speaker (5 points) 

Write one thoughtful question that you would ask the guest speaker. The question should demonstrate that you watched the lecture and considered its technical or professional implications.

### 3. Grading 

Your response should provide clear evidence that you watched the selected lecture and should go beyond a general summary.
- Evidence of lecture engagement: Accurate takeaways, appropriate timestamps, and specific references to the lecture
- Technical understanding: Correct explanation of the speaker’s ideas using appropriate groundwater terminology
- Connection to course content: Meaningful and technically supported connections to at least two course topics
- Communication and professionalism: Clear organization, professional writing, and proper attribution

### 4. Limited AI Use Permitted

Generative AI may be used as a learning aid for brainstorming, clarifying unfamiliar concepts, generating study questions, organizing ideas, outlining, proofreading, grammar, and document formatting. However, AI may not substitute for watching the lecture or produce the student’s final summary, takeaways, technical analysis, course connections, or application response. All submitted work must reflect the student’s own understanding and interpretation of the lecture. Any AI use must be acknowledged at the end of the submission by identifying the tool and briefly explaining how it was used.
